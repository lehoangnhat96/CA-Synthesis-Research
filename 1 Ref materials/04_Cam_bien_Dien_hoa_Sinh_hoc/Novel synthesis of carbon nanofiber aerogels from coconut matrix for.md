## **1. Introduction** 

Porous carbon materials have been widely used in catalysts [1–3], adsorbents [4,5] and gas storage [6–8], in regard of their high specific surface area, porosity, excellent electrical/thermal conductivity, and adjustable surface properties. Common synthesis approaches for porous carbon materials usually include hard template method, soft template method and activation method [9]. However, process difficulties including high cost, complex synthesizing procedures and low productivity are hurdling the extensive application of porous carbon materials, which further obstructs the material commercialization. Recently, carbon nanofiber aerogels (CNFs) have attracted wide attention as a new porous carbon materials [10–12]. It is a new lightweight porous multifunctional aerogel with a porosity of 80% to 99% and a specific surface 

area of 1500–2500 m[2] /g, also its surface/pore structure can be adjusted through synthesizing processes [13,14]. Comparing with traditional inorganic aerogel such as silica aerogels [15,16], CNFs which possess a higher electrochemical conductivity are more capable of the application of supercapacitors [17–19]. Furthermore, practical applications of CNFs are still extremely limited due to the complex and expensive production methods [20,21]. Therefore, synthesis pathways of CNFs with characters of environmental protection, simple procedure and easy source materials are urgently required. 

In fact, the novel cellulose aerogels based on nanofibrillated cellulose have aroused a wide research interests owing to their good biocompatibility, high specific strength and modulus, and environmentalfriendly features [22–26]. Also that CNFs can be obtained simply through high temperature calcination of the cellulose matrix of bacterial

Currently, three raw materials have been reported to fabricating cellulose based carbon aerogels, namely (i) plant cellulose, featured excellent mechanical properties, especially soft texture and high strength cotton cellulose, which possesses the best product purity and performance, a promising thermal stability [28–30]; (ii) marine biological cellulose, for example seaweed cellulose with great surface ratio, good toughness and controllable biosynthesis [31–33]; (iii) bacterial cellulose, a uniformed structure with diameter of about 10–100 nm and good biocompatibilities, shape maintenance, water permeability, high crystallinity and good tensile strength [34,35]. Most of the plants and marine organisms need to be separated and purified through complex extraction and purification process before utilizing, as to the third kind of bacterial cellulose and other related biomass materials, they can be directly adopted in the preparation of cellulose based carbon aerogels. Moreover, cellulose based carbon aerogels benefit the sustainable development of nanomaterials and carriers. The simple synthesizing procedure makes them valuable in the development and application of environmental protection materials. It has demonstrated that CNFs was one of the best matrix to well trap or immobilize enzymes for biosensing platform [36], and it has been investigated and used in electrochemical biosensors. In more details, the network of CNFs can be controlled by their diameter for optimal biosensor performance; Palladium doped CNFs biosensor can simultaneously and sensitively detect hydrogen peroxide and glucose [37]. In addition, CNFs have also exhibited the direct electron transfer (DET) of heme-proteins [38]. In brief, porous CNFs was an excellent material for superior biosensors owing to the high specific surface area, fast reactivity, good conductivity, and strong immobilization ability. 

Duo to the fast response and high sensitivity of glucose sensor made from CNFs. In this work, coconut matrix bacterial cellulose was adopted to produce porous carbon nanofibers aerogels (CMCNFs). The product was simply prepared through high temperature heat treatment (700[◦] C~1100[◦] C). Here, the highly sensitive glucose detector was synthesized and discovered that the sensitivity of the constructed sensor showed difference in various carbonization temperatures, which indicates a key factor for the selection of high sensitivity CMCNFs for glucose detection. These discovered results on the application of highly sensitive glucose detection make a significant impact for the designing of future protein-based electrochemical devices.

## _2.3. Materials characterization_ 

Powder X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns were obtained on an Rigaku Smartlab diffractometer with a Cu Kα radiation source (λ = 1.5418 Å). Field-emission scanning electron microscopy (SEM) characterization of the aerogels was performed by a Hitachi SU 4800 (Japan) with a 10 kV accelerating voltage. A transmission electron microscope (TEM Hitachi HT 7700 Japan) was used to further test the nanostructure of the samples. Brunauer-Emmett-Teller (BET) analysis from the amount of N2 absorbed at various relative vapor pressures (six points 0.05 _<_ P/P0 _<_ 0.3, nitrogen molecular cross-sectional area = 0.162 nm[2] ) was used to determine the surface area. Chemical properties of porous materials were characterized by Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR Bruker TENSOR II Germany). 

## _2.4. Electrochemical measurements_ 

Electrochemical properties of porous materials were characterized by using CHI 760E electrochemical workstation. Electrochemical studies were performed in a three electrode electrochemical cell using modified GCE as a working electrode (diameter 5 mm), Ag/AgCl electrode (Sat. KCl) as a reference electrode and platinum wire as a counter electrode. All measurements were carried out at ambient temperature and all the solutions were deoxygenated by bubbling pre-purified N2 gas for 20 min and a N2 atmosphere environment was kept in whole electrochemical measurements (except glucose catalysis). For the electrochemical test, the working electrode was prepared as below: the GCE with 5 mm diameter was polishing with 0.05 μm alumina slurry using a Buehler polishing kit, then ultrasonicated in water for 2 min and dried before use. 10 μL of CMCNFs was deposited on the pre-cleaned GCE and drying at room temperature. Then 10 μL of GOx (10 mg mL[−][1] ) was deposited on the surface of CMCNFs modified GCE and dried at ambient temperature. Then porous CMCNFs/GOx modified GCE was washed with buffer to remove loosely adsorbed enzyme. Finally 5 μL nafion solution (5%) was deposited and dried at room temperature. For comparison, GOx and CMCNFs modified GCEs were prepared following the same procedure.

## _3.3. SSA and pore structure_ 

In this study, Barrett-Joyner-Halenda (BJH) method was applied to analyze the adsorption curve to study the pore size distribution more intuitively. It is shown that the pore size and pore volume of CMCNFs are affected by the carbonization temperature. As shown in Fig. 5b and Table S1, with the increase of heat treatment temperature, the pore volume increases gradually. In more details, pore volumes of 1.25 cm[3] / g, 3.03 cm[3] /g, 3.07 cm[3] /g, 3.65 cm[3] /g and 3.76 cm[3] /g correspond to samples of BCAs, CMCNFs-700, CMCNFs-800, CMCNFs-900, CMCNFs1000, respectively. And the average pore size remains unchanged at about 3 nm followed by an almost flat tail covering mesopores region over 2– 15 nm in diameters. Specifically, the most probable distribution values of the sample decrease gradually with the increase of the treatment temperature. This may be due to the shrinkage of the sample during high temperature heat treatment, which is a common phenomenon in the aerogel materials [49–51] The N2 adsorption/ desorption 

isotherm from the sample can be clearly observed in Fig. 5a and the samples showed IV type isotherms and had capillary condensation apparently. It can be seen that the sample CMCNFs-1000 shows the hysteresis loop (typical characteristics of mesoporous materials), and its nitrogen adsorption capacity reaches as high as 4500 cm[3] /g STP, which is consistent with the large specific surface area and pore volume. Moreover, it can be inferred that SSA will be more conducive to electron transport, which will improve the sensitivity of subsequent glucose detection.

## _3.8. Selectivity, repeatability and stability of CMCNFs-1000/GOx modified electrode_ 

Selectivity is an important consideration for biosensors. The selectivity of CMCNFs-1000/GOx modified GCE was evaluated in the presence of common interfering substances (such as ascorbic acid (AA), dopamine (DA), and uric acid (UA)) in PBS. Glucose and interfering substances were consecutively added to O2-saturated PBS at a constant − applied potential of 0.4 V (Fig. S10). In Fig. 10, no significant amperometry response to the addition of interfering substances was observed. When 2 mM glucose was added, obvious current was observed. The results showed that AA, UA, and DA did not interfere with the determination of glucose. The reproducibility of the modified electrode was investigated by CV. The concentration of 3 mM glucose was measured consecutively ten times, to investigate the reproducibility of the modified electrode. The relative standard deviation (RSD) was 2.1%, indicating a good reproducibility. In addition, the stability of CMCNFs1000/GOx was examined through the response to 3 mM glucose as shown in Fig. S11. The results revealed that the cathodic peak current was still retained above 91% during seven days storage, indicating that a reasonably good stability. 

## **4. Conclusion** 

Briefly in this study, the coconut matrix carbon nanofiber aerogels CMCNFs with highly mesoporous structure were successfully prepared throw a novel method of freeze-dry and carbonized calcination (700[◦] C~1100[◦] C) of bacterial cellulose aerogels (BCAs). The CMCNFs possess ultralight low density (density ≤ 3.47 mg/cm[3] ), high specific surface area (≥1102 m[2] /g), and high porosity (≈99.6%) and achieve a direct electron transfer (DET) of GOx to the rapid detection of glucose. This character should be related to the three-dimensional pore structure determined by carbonization temperature, which has been proved that the CMCNFs calcined at 1000 degrees possesses the best capabilities. It was discovered that the good biocompatibility of the CMCNFs/GOx modified GCE exhibits a well electrocatalytic ability for the oxidation of glucose. Thus, a simple way to synthesize carbon nanofibers aerogels by using biomass is explored. The CMCNFs also has a great potential for use in enzyme glucose biofuel cells. These discovered results on the application of highly sensitive glucose detection make a significant impact for the designing of future protein-based electrochemical devices.
