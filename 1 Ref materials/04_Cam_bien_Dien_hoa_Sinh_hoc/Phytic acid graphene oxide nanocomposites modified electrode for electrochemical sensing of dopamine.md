## Phytic acid/graphene oxide nanocomposites modified electrode for electrochemical sensing of dopamine

Article history: Received 8 October 2016 Received in revised form 4 November 2016 Accepted 7 November 2016 Available online 9 November 2016 

Keywords: Dopamine Phytic acid Nanocomposites Graphene oxide Electrochemical sensor 

An electrochemical sensor for determining dopamine was developed by modifying phytic acid/graphene oxide (PA/GO) nanocomposites onto a glassy carbon electrode (GCE). PA functionalized GO was prepared by an ultra-sonication method. Subsequently, the PA/GO nanocomposites were drop-casted on a glassy carbon substrate. The structural feature of the PA/GO modified GCE was confirmed by attenuated total reflection infrared (ATR-IR) spectroscopy. The proposed electrochemical sensor was applied to detect various concentrations of DA by differential pulse voltammetry (DPV). The PA/GO/GCE was considered to be highly sensitive to DA in the range of 0.05–10 μM. In addition, the PA/GO/GCE demonstrated high electrochemical selectivity toward DA in the presence of ascorbic acid (AA) and uric acid (UA). The prepared electrochemical DA sensor was applied for detection of DA in dopamine hydrochloride injection and spiked samples of human urine with satisfactory results.

## 1. Introduction 

Dopamine (DA) is considered as one of the key neurotransmitters in the brain that implicated in a number of human pathologies [1]. Abnormal levels of DA may lead to neurological disorders, such as Parkinson's disease and schizophrenia [2]. Hence, an accurate and sensitive determination of the concentration of DA has received considerable attention. Due to its high sensitivity and simplicity, electrochemical methods are widely used for the determination of biological and environmental samples, such as DA, folic acid and glutathione et al. [3–5]. During the electro-analysis of DA, it is extremely important to avoid interferences from the co-existing compounds in the biological samples, such as ascorbic acid (AA) and uric acid (UA), whose oxidation potentials are close to that of DA [6,7]. In order to address the problems, different kinds of modified electrodes such as conducting polymer nanocomposite [7], metal oxide nanomaterial [8] and carbon material [9] modified electrodes have been developed to improve the selectivity and the sensitivity toward DA detection. 

Phytic acid (PA) is a naturally derived material from legume seeds, cereal grains, and beans [10]. Resulting from the unique properties, such as nontoxic, biocompatible, and green to the environment, PA has been attracted increasing interest for food additive, water treatment agent and corrosion prevention of metal [9]. Moreover, PA has six

## 2.1. Materials 

## 2.3. Apparatus 

Natural graphite powder was purchased from Alfa Aesar. Phytic acid (PA), dopamine (DA), uric acid (UA) and ascorbic acid (AA) were obtained from Sigma-Aldrich. Disodium hydrogen phosphate (Na2HPO4) and sodium dihydrogen phosphate (NaH2PO4) were obtained from Sinopharm Chemical Reagent (Shanghai, China). The reagents were of analytical grade, and were used without further purification. All solutions were freshly prepared using ultrapure water (N18 MΩ, Pall Corp., USA). 10 mM phosphates buffered solution (PBS, 0.5 M NaCl, pH 7.0) prepared by mixing appropriate amounts of Na2HPO4 and NaH2PO4 was employed as a supporting electrolyte during the electrochemical analysis. Stock solution of DA (5 mM) was prepared in 0.10 M PBS (pH 7.0) and diluted to give various concentrations of DA. 

All electrochemical measurements were performed on a CHI760C workstation (Chenhua Instruments, China) with a conventional threeelectrode system. The modified electrode (GCE) was used as the working electrode, a platinum plate and a saturated calomel electrode (SCE, 3 M KCl) served as the counter electrode and the reference electrode, respectively. Differential pulse voltammetry (DPV) was carried out using a step increment of 5 mV, amplitude of 50 mV, and pulse period of 0.2 s. The morphology of the prepared composites was characterized by scanning electron microscope (SEM, HITACHI-S4700). Attenuated total reflection infrared (ATR-IR) spectra were obtained by infrared spectrophotometry (Bruker, Tensor 27, Bruker Optik, Germany) with 4 cm[−][1] resolution and 100 scans, using an ATR platform by pressing the solid electrodes onto the diamond crystal.

Differential pulse voltammetry (DPV) is a sensitive technique which has been often applied for determination of DA [24,25]. Fig. 5a exhibits DPV responses of PA/GO/GCE toward the addition of DA. Resulting from the oxidation of DA to dopamine-o-quinone, a well-defined current response is observed at 0.130 V [25]. In addition, the peak current of DA increases linearly with the increasing the addition of different concentrations of DA into the electrolyte solution. The calibration curve (Fig. 5b) shows a linear increase in current density with the DA concentration from 0.05 to 10 μM (R[2] = 0.9963), the detection limit was calculated as 16 nM (S/N = 3), and the sensitivity was 4.58 μA μM[−][1] cm[−][2] , illustrating a high sensitivity of the PA/GO/GCE modified electrode to DA. Numbers of reports for the electrochemical detection of DA are compared with the present work, as listed in Table 1. It is found that the developed electrochemical sensor had a wide linear range and low detection limit, which may be attributed to the synergistic effect of PA and GO nanocomposites. Compared with the electrochemical DA sensors based on metal or metal oxide nanostructures modified electrode, our sensor may not show a lower limit of detection or wider detection range. But, through the contrast test by the representative diagnostic detection of DA in the hospital, all of the results are acceptable. Moreover, our strategy represents a simple one-pot preparation of PA/GO hybrids for DA sensing with a biocompatible, nontoxic and environment friendly natural material. 

Five different PA/GO nanocomposites modified electrodes were investigated by DPV measurement of 5 μM DA in 10 mM PBS (pH 7.0). The relative standard deviation (RSD) of peak current is calculated as 4.7%. After testing, the modified electrode was rinsed with PBS (pH 7.0) and stored at 4 °C, and the electrochemical response of 5 μM DA lost only 5.9% of the initial response after two weeks. These results indicate that the modified electrode has good reproducibility and stability.

## 4. Conclusion 

In this paper, determination of DA in the presence of AA and UA was achieved by using a PA/GO nanocomposites modified GCE. Due to the synergistic effect of the PA/GO nanocomposites, the fabricated sensor exhibited high electro-catalytic activities toward the detection of DA, and the results confirmed the suitability of the PA/GO/GCE as an effective sensing strategy for selective DA determination in the presence of AA and UA. The electrochemical performance of the PA/GO/GCE was investigated by DPV technique, and the results revealed that the sensor has highly sensitivity to detect DA in the range of 0.05–10 μM, and the sensor also displayed good reproducibility and stability. Moreover, the availability of the PA/GO nanocomposites can be successfully used for the detection of DA in dopamine hydrochloride injection and human urine samples.
