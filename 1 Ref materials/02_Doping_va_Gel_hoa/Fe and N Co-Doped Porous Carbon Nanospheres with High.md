**Abstract:** To enhance the electrocatalytic performance of transition metal and nitrogen codoped carbon (M-N-C) for CO2 electro-reduction reaction, we present a Fe and N co-doped porous carbon nanosphere (Fe-N-PC) with high density of single-atom iron active sites and a few Fe clusters, as indicated by high-resolution TEM and EXAFS, using SiO2 as protecting shell to restrict isolated Fe atoms and to trap the volatile N-containing species. The Fe doping content can reach as high as 3.9 wt. %. The Fe-N-PC catalyst exhibits an excellent performance 

for converting CO2 to CO with high faradic efficiency (~90 %) and remarkable partial CO current density (11.44 mA cm[-2] ) at -0.49 V. Probing analysis and selective chemical modification reveal that both Fe atom and ortho-C of N atoms play a synergetic role in enhancing CO2RR activity. DFT calculations reveal that the presence of amorphous Fe clusters can facilitate the desorption of CO from the surface of the catalyst, thus further improving the CO2RR activity. This work presents a general strategy to synthesize advanced M-N-C material with high density of single atom active sites, which could be used as an efficient electrocatalyst for CO2RR, ORR et al.

## **1. Introduction** 

CO2 electro-reduction reaction (CO2RR) has received extensive attentions due to ambient reaction conditions and easily controllable high value-added products. Since the CO2RR suffers from a high overpotential, it is often accompanied by the hydrogen evolution reaction (HER), leading to a poor selectivity of the target products.[1-2] In the past few years, a series of catalysts, such as metals,[3-4] metallic oxides,[5] metal alloys,[6] etc., have been reported in order to improve the efficiency of CO2RR. For instance, metallic Au,[7-9] Ag[10] or Pd[11] catalysts have been found to be able to catalyze electroreduction of CO2 to CO and HCOOH. However, the insufficient activity and low selectivity, as well as the prohibitive price of these catalysts severely impede the future applications. 

Transition metal and nitrogen co-doped carbon (M-N-C) materials have been recently considered as promising substitutes for noble metal catalysts due to their low cost, high surface area, tunable structure, and unique electronic feature. A variety of M-N-C materials, including carbon nanotubes,[12] carbon nanospheres,[13] and graphene[14] , have emerged as electrocatalysts for CO2RR in recent years. For example, Strasser et al.[15] developed a variety of M−N−C catalysts (M=Mn, Fe, Co, Cu, Ni) to study the effects of transition metal on CO2RR performance. They have found that the Ni-N-C catalyst presented the highest current for CO formation but at very high potentials (<-0.75 V vs. RHE) and that the Fe-N-C was identified to obviously decrease the overpotential of CO2RR. As reported, the introduction of metallic Fe nanoparticles within Fe-N-C materials could further reduce the overpotential.[16] Our previous work[17] has also demonstrated that the FeXN nanoparticles encapsulated within Fe and N co-doped carbon layers can further promote the activity for the conversion of CO2 to CO at a low overpotential, but there is a great space for the current improvement. The aggregation of transition metal within M-N-C precursors, usually occurring during the heattreatment at high temperature, would be favorable for the hydrogen evolution reaction (HER) rather than the CO2RR.[16, 18-19] Therefore, it is crucial to develop a strategy that can restrain the aggregation of Fe and increase the density of Fe−N active sites. To this end, the understanding of the active sites is of great significance to the development of novel M-N-C catalysts. 

In this work, we develop a strategy to synthesize mesoporous Fe and N co-doped porous carbon nanospheres (Fe-N-PC) with high density of atomic Fe active sites and a few Fe clusters, employing SiO2 as template and protecting shell. Simultaneously，Fe precursors were trapped into polymer network through the electrostatic interaction. SiO2 shell[22-23] acts as a microreactor to prevent aggregation of isolated Fe atoms and volatilization of N-containing species during the formation of Fe-N-C materials at high temperature. The optimal Fe−N−PC catalyst exhibits excellent activity for electroreduction of CO2 to CO with high faradic efficiency (FE) of 90 % and partial CO current density of 11.44 mA cm[-2] at -0.49 V versus reversible hydrogen electrode (RHE). The poisoning experiment and selective chemical modification verify that both Fe atom and ortho-C of pyridinic N are conducive to improve the CO2RR activity. Theoretical calculations demonstrate that amorphous Fe clusters can dramatically lower CO bonding strength, thus facilitating the CO2RR activity of Fe-Nx active sites.

## **2.1. Synthesis of Fe-N-PC** 

1.5 g of SiO2 nanosphere (25 nm) was dispersed in 35 mL of deionized water, and then 900 μL of pyrrole was injected into the SiO2 suspensions under vigorous stirring. After that, 15 mL of aqueous solution containing 0.65 g of ammonium thiosulphate ((NH4)2S2O4) and 0.78 g of potassium ferricyanide was poured into the above mixture under stirring and reacted in ice bath for 24 h. After centrifugation, washing with water and drying, the obtained SiO2@PPyFe(CN)6 was dispersed in 90 mL of ethanol under sonication and vigorous stirring, followed by injection of 10 mL of ammonium hydroxide (NH3·H2O, 28 wt. %). Then, 4 mL of tetraethylorthosilicate (TEOS) was dropwise added to the mixture and reacted at room temperature for 24 h. The product (SiO2@PPy-Fe(CN)6@SiO2) was collected by rotary evaporation and ground into fine powders. The precursors were then pyrolyzed at 900 °C for 2 

h with a heating rate of 5 °C min[−1 ] under N2 protection. After cooling to room temperature, the sample was immersed in 4 mol·L[-1] NaOH solution under stirring at 80 °C for 24 h to etch the SiO2, followed by centrifugation, washing with 1 mol·L[-1] NaOH and water several times and vacuum drying at 60 °C. Finally, the sample was annealed to 900 °C under N2 protection with a rate of 5 °C min[-1] and held for 10 min in NH3 with a flow rate of 200 mL min[-1] . Then the sample was cooled to room temperature protected by N2 naturally to get the Fe-N-PC. 

For a comparison, the samples without the addition of NH3·H2O and TEOS to form SiO2 shell (denoted as op-Fe-N-PC) and without the addition of Fe (denoted as N-PC) were also synthesized. 

To achieve selective chemical modification, the best Fe-N-PC (100 mg) was dispersed in 

5 mL of ethanol and 5 mL of 40 % aldehyde aqueous solution. 100 mg of [Bis(trifluoroacetoxy)iodo]-benzene was added and the mixture was stirred under room

## **2.2. Physical characterizations** 

The morphologies were characterized by transmission electron microscopy (TEM, FEI Tencnai 30F, 200 kV) and high angle annular dark field scanning transmission electron microscopy (HADDF-STEM, FEI Titan Themis G2 60-300). The crystalline structure was characterized by X-ray diffraction (XRD, Bruker AXS D8 ADVANCE). The chemical states and compositions were measured by X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS, Thermo Fisher Escalab 250 Xi). The surface areas and porosities were determined by measuring the N2 adsorption-desorption isotherms on Micromeritics ASAP-2020 instrument. The X-ray absorption fine structure (XAFS) analysis was conducted on BL14W1 beamline of Shanghai synchrotron radiation facility. Liquid product was characterized by[1] H NMR on Bruker AVANCE AV III 400. Function group of sample was characterized by fourier transform infrared spectrometer (FTIR, Thermo Fisher Nicolet IS5).

## **3. Results and discussion** 

The synthesis of Fe-N-PC is schematic illustrated in Scheme 1. Firstly, the polymerization of pyrrole monomers is triggered with (NH4)2S2O4 as oxidant to form PPy layers coating on SiO2 nanosphere. The Fe(CN)6[3-] dissolved in the solution can be assembled into PPy network through the electrostatic interaction because of the positively charged groups of PPy chain. The coating of outer SiO2 shell was prepared by hydrolyzing TEOS with NH3·H2O[33] , which is able to prevent the migration of Fe atoms and volatilization of N-containing species. After pyrolysis at 900 °C under N2, etching the SiO2 with NaOH and second heat-treatment under NH3, the final Fe-N-PC catalysts can be obtained with the porous nanosphere structure. For the sake of 

comparison, the op-Fe-N-PC without the protection of SiO2 shell was also synthesized.

X-ray adsorption near edge structure (XANES) and extended X-ray adsorption fine structure (EXAFS) spectroscopy were carried out to unravel the chemical status and coordination environment of Fe in Fe-N-PC. The XANES curves of Fe-N-PC and op-Fe-N-PC show near-edge absorption energy between those of iron(Ⅱ) phthalocyanine (Fe[Ⅱ] Pc) and Fe foil, implying that Fe atoms carry positive charges (Figure 2a). As shown in Figure 2b, the experimental Fourier transform (FT) at Fe K-edge EXAFS exhibits an obvious peak at ~1.5 Å 

for both Fe-N-PC and op-Fe-N-PC, which can be attributed to Fe-N(O) scattering paths, matched well with the peak of Fe[Ⅱ] Pc. Interestingly, the EXAFS spectrum of Fe-N-PC displays a weak peak at around 2.1 Å, which can be assigned as Fe-Fe bond. Combined with the HAADF-STEM observation, a few Fe clusters might exist in Fe-N-PC, which are probably formed during NH3 heat-treatment. By contrast, the FT-EXAFS curve of the op-Fe-N-PC sample presents a high peak at 2.1 Å, indicative of the presence of much more Fe-Fe bond. These results clearly demonstrate the critical role of SiO2 shell in restricting isolated Fe atoms from aggregation, hence greatly improving the density of atomic Fe active sites.

The electroreduction of CO2 to CO contains three elementary steps and two important intermediates, which are COOH* and CO*. In conclusion, we present a facile route to prepare Fe and N co-doped porous carbon nanospheres with the high density of atomic Fe-N4-C active sites and a few Fe clusters using SiO2 as template and protecting shell. The introduction of SiO2 shell conduces to improve content of Fe (3.9 wt. %) and N (7.82 at. %) in final catalyst. The best performing Fe-N-PC catalyst exhibits outstanding catalytic activity for electrochemical reduction of CO2 to CO result from high density of active sites. The poisoning experiment and selective chemical modification demonstrate that the active site for CO2RR probably include both of the Fe-N4 center and the surrounding carbon atoms. Comparative experiments and DFT simulations further confirm that the Fe clusters further promote the CO2RR activity due to the low free energy of CO desorption from Fe-N4-C active sites. The synthesis strategy and the exploration of active sites might contribute to the rational design of highly active M-N-C catalysts.
