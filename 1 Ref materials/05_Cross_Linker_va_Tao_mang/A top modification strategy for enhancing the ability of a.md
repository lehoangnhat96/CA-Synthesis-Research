Chitosan is a promising substitute for heavy metal ion adsorbents. However, traditional pure chitosan adsorbents have certain disadvantages that limit their application. In this paper, a ‘top modification’ strategy was used to enhance the capturing ability of chitosan adsorbents. A chitosan aerogel was prepared via physical crosslinking and then enhanced by immersion in ethylenediamine tetraacetic anhydride solution. Finally, an enhanced chitosan aerogel was obtained, and analyses were used to describe its structure, adsorption properties and mechanism. Results showed that both the porous structure and the combined complexations dramatically improved the capturing ability of the chitosan aerogel for heavy metal ions. The theoretical adsorption capacities of the enhanced aerogel for Cu[2+] , Pb[2+] and Cd[2+] reached 108.14, 143.73 and 84.62 mg/g, respectively. Due to their environmental friendliness, good adsorption performance, easy separation and reusability, enhanced aerogels have become viable solutions to removing heavy metal pollutants from aquatic systems.

Currently, heavy metal ions have become a primary factor that causes water pollution. These heavy metal ions are not degradable and tend to accumulate in biological tissues via the food chain in water ecosystem systems, causing serious diseases and harming human health [1]. Methods to reduce heavy metal ions in water include adsorption [2], precipitation [3], nanofiltration [4] and oxidation [5]. Adsorption is considered a promising water treatment technology due to its simple operation, low energy consumption, low cost, and renewable adsorbents [6]. Mineral adsorbents [7] and nanoparticle adsorbents [8] have been studied extensively; however, most exhibit low reusability and cause secondary harm to the environment. To achieve efficient treatment of wastewater and reduce secondary pollution, researchers have been investigating environmentally friendly chitosan (CS) adsorbents [9–16]. 

Chitosan is the only alkaline polysaccharide in nature and contains many hydroxyl groups and amino groups that have high activities for heavy metal ions [17]. However, traditional pure chitosan adsorbents exhibit low porosity and are easily lost and unstable in acidic media, limiting their application [18]. The rich porous structure of aerogels can be used to compensate for these defects. Aerogels with high porosity capture heavy metal ions more easily. Concurrently, compared to powdery chitosan adsorbents, blocky aerogels are more promising because they can be easily separated and recovered from aquatic systems. However, chemical crosslinking, which is traditionally used to prepare CS aerogels [9,19,20], typically reduces the number of active sites for heavy metal ions, which is a primary reason for the low capturing ability of pure chitosan adsorbents. To improve adsorption efficiency, researchers constructed chitosan hydrogels via physical crosslinking [21]. This method effectively mitigates the consumption of adsorption sites; however, weak physical interactions lead to lower structural stability, which limits its reusability. In this study, to enhance the capturing ability of pure chitosan adsorbent, ethylenediamine tetraacetic anhydride (EDTAA) is used to increase the number of active sites via a simple immersion method after blocky chitosan aerogel was prepared via physical crosslinking. The investigation was performed on the structure, adsorption properties and mechanism of the enhanced chitosan aerogel. This study provides a relatively easy and efficient ‘top modification’ strategy to create an enhanced pure chitosan adsorbent for heavy metal ions.

## 2.6. Regeneration of E-CS aerogel 

To evaluate the regeneration ability of the E-CS aerogel, the metal ion-loaded samples were desorbed and regenerated with the pickling method [27]. 

E-CS aerogel (0.1 g) was added to a conical flask containing 100 ml heavy metal ion solution (50 mg/L), and the solution was shaken at 150 rpm for 12 h. Then, the E-CS aerogel was removed to measure the removal efficiency. Then, the metal ion-loaded E- CS aerogel was placed in a conical flask containing 100 ml 0.5 M HCl solution and shaken at 150 rpm for 12 h to regenerate. The refreshed E-CS aerogel was obtained after freeze-drying again and was used to measure the removal efficiency in the next cycle. 

## 3. Results and discussion 

## 3.1. Enhancement mechanism of ethylenediamine tetraacetic anhydride for chitosan aerogel 

As a promising candidate for heavy metal ion adsorbents, chitosan molecules contain many hydroxyl groups and amino groups with high activity for heavy metal ions. There are two ways to build CS aerogel’s structure: chemical crosslinking and physical crosslinking. Typically, to form a stable 3D network structure, chemical reactions occur between crosslinking agents and active groups of chitosan during chemical crosslinking. These reactions consume many active groups of the chitosan, which play a major role in capturing heavy metal ions and lead to degradation of the adsorption properties. In this study, physical crosslinking was used to retain as many active groups as possible. The resulting physicalcrosslinking hydrogel was created with hydrogen-bonding interactions between the chitosan molecules. While heating up in the solvent, the chitosan molecules reform, such as chains curling and winding, which can result in intensive hydrogen-bonding interactions. Under the synthetic influence of hydrophobic interactions and hydrogen-bonding interactions, a physically crosslinked hydrogel was formed without the loss of active groups. After lyophilization, a CS aerogel with as many active groups as possible was created. 

To improve the capturing ability to heavy metal ions using a relatively easy and efficient ‘top modification’ strategy, the CS aerogel was enhanced via an acylation reaction between the amino groups of chitosan and carboxylic groups of EDTAA in this study. In theory, one amino group of the chitosan molecule could exchange for three carboxylic groups of EDTAA, as shown in Scheme 1; these groups become new active sites for heavy metal ions. Considering that one EDTAA molecule could react with more than one amino group, it was reasonable to infer that there was a bridge effect to make the aerogel’s structure more stable.

## 4. Conclusions 

In this study, a blocky pure chitosan aerogel adsorbent was prepared and enhanced successfully via physical crosslinking and acylation with ethylenediamine tetraacetic anhydride under a relatively easy and efficient ‘top modification’ strategy. This strategy ensured a porous structure of the adsorbent and effectively increased the number of active sites. The capturing ability of the chitosan adsorbent for heavy metal ions was improved dramatically. The saturated adsorption capacities of the E-CS aerogel for Cu[2+] , Pb[2+] and Cd[2+] reached 108.14, 143.73 and 84.62 mg/g, respectively. Investigation of the adsorption mechanism showed that the adsorption process was described accurately by the pseudo second-order kinetic model and Langmuir isotherm model. A comprehensive interaction between Cu[2+] and the E-CS aerogel occurred, including the combined complexation of amino groups and carbonyl groups and the physical adsorption of porous structures. With their environmental friendliness, good adsorption performances, and easy separation and reusability, E-CS aerogels are attractive for removing heavy metal pollutants from aquatic systems. This ‘top modification’ strategy provides a valuable pathway towards achieving efficient absorbents for heavy metal ions.
