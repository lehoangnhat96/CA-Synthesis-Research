The widespread deployment of renewable energy sources worldwide, such as wind power and 
photovoltaics, has created an urgent need for eﬃcient energy storage systems. Biomass-derived 
carbon aerogels, due to their environmentally friendly and sustainable properties, have emerged 
as ideal precursor materials for advanced energy storage applications, particularly in superca- 
pacitors. This study developed a method to prepare phytate-induced phosphorus/sulfur (P/S) 
co-doped magnesium lignosulfonate-based carbon aerogels (LCAs). Phytate induction facilitated 
the formation of regular spherical structures while simultaneously optimizing surface morphol- 
ogy and enabling eﬃcient and uniform doping of P and S heteroatoms. The optimized sam- 
ple, LCA-2-700, the lignin-based carbon aerogel that was prepared with the magnesium ligno- 
sulfonate (LS) ꞉sodium alginate (SA) ꞉phytic acid (PA) mass ratio of 5 ꞉5 ꞉2 and carbonized at 700 
°C, exhibited a speciﬁc capacitance of 362 F/g at the current density of 0.5 A/g, with the as- 
sembled device achieving an energy density of 40.1 W·h/kg at a power density of 700 W/kg. 
After 20,000 cycles, the capacitance retention rate remained at 82.5%, demonstrating excel- 
lent electrochemical durability. The high performance was attributed to the synergistic eﬀects 
of its spherical structure, high speciﬁc surface area, and P/S dual-heteroatom doping. This study 
provides an eﬀective approach for synergistic structure-doping regulation of lignin-based car- 
bon aerogels and provides a potential pathway for practical applications in high-performance 
supercapacitors.

The functional groups present in the carbon aerogel were further characterized by FT-IR spectroscopy as shown in Fig. 2 d. The ab- 
sorption bands between 1696 and 1635 cm–1 were assigned to stretching vibrations of the aromatic benzene skeleton. Additionally,

lignosulphonate at temperatures of 600–800 °C, the –SO3 H group underwent almost complete decomposition. 
The pyrolytic behavior of LCA at the elevated temperatures was evaluated by thermogravimetric analysis. As depicted in Figs. 2 e 
and 2 f, the mass loss during heating from ambient temperature to 650 °C occurred in two distinct stages. The ﬁrst stage, between 
room temperature and 200 °C, showed a mass loss of approximately 20%, primarily due to the evaporation of adsorbed water. 
This hygroscopicity originated from the abundance of carboxyl and hydroxyl groups in magnesium lignosulfonate and SA. As the 
temperature reached 100 °C, the release of absorbed water and volatile components occurred. The second mass loss stage, observed 
between 200 and 450 °C, exhibited a pronounced peak rate at around 275 °C, which was associated with the decomposition of 
magnesium lignosulfonate and SA, leading to the formation of a stable carbonaceous matrix. It was also found that the introduction 
of PA played a key protective role during pyrolysis. Owing to the corrosion resistance of PA and the release of low-boiling-point 
molecules, the initial decomposition temperature of the aerogel was reduced, thereby accelerating the early thermal degradation 
process and enhancing the overall thermal stability. Furthermore, the presence of PA facilitated the consolidation of the carbon 
architecture, which was critical for constructing a robust aerogel framework and provided a solid basis for its potential application 
in high-temperature environments. 
Figs. 3a–3d present the FE-SEM images of LCA-700, LCA-1-700, LCA-2-700, and LCA-3-700. It could be observed that the surface 
of LCA-700 exhibited a rough and disordered morphology. With the incorporation of PA, the materials progressively develop a well- 
deﬁned spherical architecture. The formation of spherical structures upon PA addition could be attributed to the coordination between 
PA and metal ions in LCA, which promoted self-assembly during gelation. The amount of PA inﬂuenced the size and uniformity of 
the spheres, as evidenced by FE-SEM images ( Figs. 3a–3d ). Based on the speciﬁc capacitance data for diﬀerent carbon aerogels 
provided in Table S1 and Fig. S1, where LCA-2-700 exhibited the highest speciﬁc capacitance, we hypothesised that its outstanding 
electrochemical performance was closely linked to its pronounced spherical layered structure. When the temperature increased to 800 
°C ( Fig. 3 f), the spherical architecture was retained but appeared to undergo partial fusion, resulting in less distinct inter-spherical 
boundaries and a reduction in the ﬁne surface texture of the layers compared to LCA-2-700. This structural coarsening was consistent 
with the notable decrease in speciﬁc surface area and pore volume observed for LCA-2-800 (Table S2), likely due to partial collapse 
and sintering of the carbon framework at the elevated temperature. Based on the data summarized in Table S1, it could be inferred 
that both a fully developed spherical morphology and a smooth layered structure were crucial for enhancing the energy storage 
performance of the material.

The pore structure of the carbon aerogels was further characterized by N2 adsorption-desorption measurements as shown in Figs. 4 a 
and 4 b. The isotherms of LCA-2-600, LCA-2-700, and LCA-2-800 all exhibited Ⅳ -type curves. A pronounced uptake at low relative 
pressure ( P / P0 < 0.01) suggested the presence of micropores in all three samples. Notably, within the medium relative pressure range 
(0.4 < P / P0 < 0.9), LCA-2-700 showed an H3-type hysteresis loop, indicating the existence of a uniform mesoporous structure with 
a pore size distribution centered between 2–10 nm. As summarized in Table S3, the speciﬁc surface areas of LCA-2-600, LCA-2-700, 
and LCA-2-800 are 170.117, 273.107, and 196.835 m2 /g, respectively, with corresponding average pore sizes of 1.69 nm, 4.74 nm, 
and 1.86 nm, respectively. While LCA-2-600 and LCA-2-800 were predominantly microporous, LCA-2-700 exhibited a mesoporous- 
dominated structure, which was more conducive to electrochemical energy storage applications ( Liu et al., 2016 ; Xiao et al., 2020 ; 
Kong and Wang, 2021 ). 
As shown in Fig. 4 c, all samples exhibited two characteristic peaks located at approximately 1350 (D band) and 1580 cm–1 (G 
band). The D band is associated with structural defects and disordered carbon domains, while the G band corresponds to the in-plane 
vibration of sp2 -hybridized graphitic carbon. The intensities of the G and D bands were determined by Gaussian ﬁtting, and the 
degree of graphitization was evaluated based on the intensity ratio ( ID / IG , where ID is the intensity of D-band, IG is the intensity of 
G-band) ( Zhao et al., 2015 ; Kong and Wang, 2021 ). The IG / ID values for LCA-2-600, LCA-2-700, and LCA-2-800 were calculated to be 
2.9, 2.8, and 2.7, respectively, indicating a slight increase in graphitization with the rise of temperature, although the overall change 
remained moderate. The structural properties of the samples were further investigated by X-ray diﬀraction (XRD). As depicted in 
Fig. 4 d, the XRD patterns displayed broad and weak diﬀraction peaks at around 23° and 43.8°, corresponding to the (002) and (100) 
crystal planes of carbon, respectively, suggesting a predominantly amorphous structure.

The exceptional performance of LCA-2-700 stemmed from the synergistic cooperation between its hierarchical pore structure 
and heteroatom functionality. The mesopore-dominated architecture (average pore size of 4.74 nm) provided eﬃcient pathways for 
rapid ion diﬀusion, ensuring timely access of electrolyte ions to active sites. Meanwhile, the uniformly distributed P/S heteroatoms 
created abundant electrochemically active centers on the carbon surface. This synergy enabled both eﬃcient electric double-layer 
formation in the mesopores and enhanced pseudocapacitance through surface redox reactions, overcoming the kinetic limitations 
typically encountered in microporous carbon materials. The electrochemical performance was then evaluated. 
Electrochemical tests were also conducted in 1 mol/L H2 SO4 to evaluate performance in acidic media, where the protonation of P 
and S functional groups could be favourable. However, the speciﬁc capacitances measured in H2 SO4 were lower than those in KOH 
across all current densities, as shown in Fig. S3. Therefore, KOH was selected as the electrolyte for all subsequent tests. 
The CV curves of LCA-2-600, LCA-2-700, and LCA-2-800 at the scanning rate of 20 mV/s were shown in Fig. 6 a. Each CV curve pre- 
sented a wide peak in the potential range from –0.6 to –0.8 V, indicating that the induced heteroatoms brought pseudo-capacitance be- 
haviour, and the reversible redox reaction related to the doped functional groups of heteroatoms was an observed pseudo-capacitance 
characteristic, which was the result of the co-existence of pseudocapacitance and double-layer capacitance ( Cheng et al., 2020 ). As 
the activation temperature increased from 600 to 700 °C, the area under the CV curve (a measure of electrochemical energy storage 
capacity) increased signiﬁcantly. However, further increasing the activation temperature to 800 °C resulted in a decrease in the area 
under the CV curve. As a result, LCA-2-700 showed the best energy storage performance in the test samples. 
As depicted in Fig. 6 b, the constant-current charge-discharge (GCD) curves of LCA-2-600, LCA-2-700, and LCA-2-800 at a current 
density of 1 A/g tended to form isosceles triangles, signifying the synergistic contribution of both double-layer capacitance (EDLC) and 
pseudocapacitive processes ( Cao et al., 2020 ). The energy storage results obtained from the GCD test were in agreement with those 
obtained from the CV tests. The speciﬁc capacitance of LCA-2-600, LCA-2-700, and LCA-2-800 at a current density of 1 A/g is 131, 
335, and 254 F/g, respectively. In addition, as shown in Fig. 6 c, the capacitance retention of LCA-2-700 reached 77.3%, demonstrating 
good electrochemical stability. It still showed excellent magniﬁcation performance even at a current density of 10 A/g because of the 
pseudocapacitance brought about by heteroatom doping. This doping strategy eﬀectively optimized the electrochemical properties 
of the material, enabling it to maintain a high capacitance value and good cycle stability during the high-current charge-discharge 
process, further proving the potential application value of LCA-2-700 in high-performance energy storage devices.

In addition, electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) analysis was performed in the range of 10 mHz–100 kHz to gain an 
in-depth understanding of the charge transport mechanics of the electrode materials. As shown in Fig. 6 f, the EIS curves of carbon 
aerogel for LCA-2-600, LCA-2-700, and LCA-2-800 consisted of two diﬀerent parts: the semicircle of the high frequency region, which 
corresponded to the charge transfer resistance between electrolyte ions and electrons; a straight line in the low frequency region, 
indicating the diﬀusion and migration of electrolyte ions within the porous structure. Through careful observation of the EIS curve, 
we could ﬁnd that the LCA-2-700 line was closer to the 45 °C angle in the low frequency region, which clearly showed its excellent 
ion diﬀusion performance. In addition, after ﬁtting the impedance of LCA-2-700 in the high frequency region, a very low value of 
0.0045 Ω was obtained, which was undoubtedly conducive to the rapid charge transport. The impedance value, as a key parameter 
to evaluate the charge transfer characteristics of the material, further conﬁrmed the excellent performance of LCA-2-700 in terms of 
electrochemical performance. 
The CV curves of LCA-2-700, ranging from 5 to 100 mV/s, were depicted in Fig. 6 d. These curves consistently demonstrated 
superior morphological stability, which was attributed to the rapid charge transfer kinetics at the electrode/electrolyte interface. 
The charge storage mechanism of LCA-2-700 was further elucidated by assessing the contributions of current to its surface and 
diﬀusion-controlled processes. The capacitive contribution of the LCA-2-700 electrode was quantiﬁed using the Dunn equation, and 
the parameter b (slope of the line in Fig. 6 g) depicted in Fig. 6 g characterized the electrochemical processes. A value of b = 1 signiﬁes a 
predominantly surface-controlled capacitive behaviour, whereas b = 0.5 indicates a diﬀusion-controlled process, typically associated 
with Faradaic reactions ( Huang et al., 2023 ; Liu et al., 2023 ). Values of b between 0.5 and 1.0 suggest a hybrid mechanism. The b 
values obtained from our analysis conﬁrmed the mixed charge storage mechanism of LCA-2-700. As shown in Fig. 6 i, the capacitance 
of LCA-2-700 showed a tendency to decrease with the increase of the scanning rate. At the same time, the contribution rate of 
pseudocapacitance storage to the entire thin ﬁlm capacitance increased signiﬁcantly from 59% to 88%, which clearly indicated that 
the pseudo-capacitance eﬀect caused by heteroatom doping played a dominant role in the entire capacitance mechanism, and thus 
strongly conﬁrmed the successful implementation of the heteroatom doping strategy. 
The LCA-2-700 electrodes within a symmetric two-electrode conﬁguration were employed for practical applications, utilizing LCA- 
2-700 for both the positive and negative electrode materials and a 6 mol/L aqueous potassium hydroxide solution as the electrolyte 
to construct a symmetric supercapacitor device ( Fig. 7 a). Fig. 7 b presents the CV curves of the LCA-2-700//LCA-2-700 SSC across 
various voltage windows. Due to the successful doping of heteroatoms, at a scanning rate of 50 mV/s, the CV curves maintained a 
rectangular shape even when the voltage window was expanded from 1 V to 1.4 V. This indicates a broad potential window and the 
capability to deliver a higher energy density compared to systems with narrower windows.

sity of the previous reported lignin-based carbon supercapacitor devices with a two-electrode system ( Wahid et al., 2014 ; Huang et al., 
2016 ; Salinas-Torres et al., 2016 ; Gong et al., 2017 ; Huang et al., 2017 ; Hao, et al., 2019 ; Fu et al., 2020 ; Du et al., 2021 ). 
4. Conclusions 
This study presented a simple and eﬃcient strategy for synthesizing phosphorus/sulfur (P/S) co-doped carbon aerogels using phytic 
acid (PA), magnesium lignosulfonate (LS), and sodium alginate (SA) as sustainable precursors. The innovative approach leveraged 
the inherent sulfur in magnesium lignosulfonate for in-situ self-doping and utilized PA as both a phosphorus source and a morphology 
regulator. We have demonstrated that the superior electrochemical performance originated from the synergistic interplay between 
the spherical mesoporous architecture and P/S dual-doping. The mesoporous framework facilitated ion transport kinetics, while the 
heteroatom functionalities provided substantial pseudocapacitance through speciﬁc redox reactions involving P = O/P–O and C–S–C 
groups in alkaline electrolyte. Performance testing conﬁrmed that the optimized LCA-2-700 sample exhibited a remarkable speciﬁc 
capacitance of 362 F/g at the current density of 0.5 A/g. The assembled symmetrical supercapacitor achieved a high energy density 
of 40.1 W·h/kg at a power density of 700 W/kg and maintains 82.5% capacitance retention after 20,000 cycles. The high performance 
is attributed to the synergistic eﬀect of the spherical structure and P/S dual-heteroatom doping. This work not only demonstrates the 
high-value utilization of biomass waste but also provides a promising electrode material for advanced supercapacitors.
