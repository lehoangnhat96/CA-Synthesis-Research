## **Identifying the Key Role of Pyridinic-N–Co Bonding in Synergistic Electrocatalysis for Reversible ORR/OER** 

_Xue-Rui Wang, Jie-Yu Liu, Zi-Wei Liu, Wei-Chao Wang,* Jun Luo, Xiao-Peng Han, Xi-Wen Du, Shi-Zhang Qiao, and Jing Yang*_

In addition, it is of great challenges to rationally design and exploit efficient bifunctional ORR/OER electrocatalysts, necessary to the regenerative operation.[[4]] 

**For many regenerative electrochemical energy-conversion systems, hybrid electrocatalysts comprising transition metal (TM) oxides and heteroatomdoped (e.g., nitrogen-doped) carbonaceous materials are promising bifunctional oxygen reduction reaction/oxygen evolution reaction electrocatalysts, whose enhanced electrocatalytic activities are attributed to the synergistic effect originated from the TM–N–C active sites. However, it is still ambiguous which configuration of nitrogen dopants, either pyridinic or pyrrolic N, when bonded to the TM in oxides, predominately contributes to the synergistic effect. Herein, an innovative strategy based on laser irradiation is described to controllably tune the relative concentrations of pyridinic and pyrrolic nitrogen dopants in the hybrid catalyst, i.e., NiCo2O4 NPs/N-doped mesoporous graphene. Comparative studies reveal the dominant role of pyridinic-N**  **Co bonding, instead of pyrrolic-N bonding, in synergistically promoting reversible oxygen electrocatalysis. Moreover, density functional theory calculations provide deep insights into the corresponding synergistic mechanism. The optimized hybrid, NiCo/NLG-270, manifests outstanding reversible oxygen electrocatalytic activities, leading to an overpotential different Δ** _**E**_ **among the lowest value for highly efficient bifunctional catalysts. In a practical reversible Zn–air battery, NiCo/NLG-270 exhibits superior charge/discharge performance and long-term durability compared to the noble metal electrocatalysts.** 

Numbers of recent studies have shown that hybrid electrocatalysts, composed of earth-abundant transition metal (TM) oxides and heteroatom-doped (e.g., nitrogen-doped) carbonaceous materials, usually exhibit exceptional bifunctional ORR/OER activities and good stability superior to their single-component counterparts.[[4a,5]] The improved electro- 

chemical performance of the composites has been generally ascribed to the followings. (1) The strong chemical bonding between metal-containing species and N-doped-carbon supports, due to the high affinity of nitrogen (especially pyridinic and pyrrolic N) to the TM ions, can greatly facilitate the interfacial electron transfer for the hybrid electrocatalysts.[[4a,6]]

(2) More importantly, the strong coupling between TM and N–carbon at the interface can normally induce a so-called synergistic effect for the hybrids,[[5a,7]] benefiting substantially enhanced oxygen electrochemistry compared to their counterparts without nitrogen doping. Nevertheless, up to date, the fundamental mechanism of synergistic electrocatalysis for ORR/OER is still ambiguous. In particular, although it = was evidenced that the M–N–C (M Fe, Co, Cu, etc.) active sites played an important role in boosting the electrocatalytic activities of metal-oxide/N–carbon hybrids,[[4a,5a,c,8]] little has been done to distinguish the contributions from MN bonds with different nitrogen configurations. In other words, it is still unclear which configuration of nitrogen dopants, either pyridinic or pyrrolic N, when bonded to TM in oxides, predominately contributes to the synergistic effect, and how it would cooperate with the metal ions at the hybrid interface to synergistically catalyze both oxygen reduction and evolution processes. Recently, Yu et al. speculated that the pyridinic N coordinated with iron in γ-Fe2O3/N-doped carbon nanotubes was the primary ORR active site.[[9]] However, the contribution from pyrrolic N was not ruled out, moreover, for other catalytically active TM ions, e.g., Co or Ni which are normally active sites not only for ORR but also for OER, it is still unknown which configuration of nitrogen dopants will help to synergistically promote the reversible oxygen electrocatalysis for the hybrids. The research bottle-neck essentially lies in the fact that it is rather hard to selectively tune the concentration of nitrogen dopants with different configurations. It was reported recently that thermal treatment of a mixture of iron precursors and rGO under ammonia gas would tune the concentration of pyridinic N by varying the annealing time at 900 °C, whereas the high-temperature annealing unavoidably resulted 

in large NPs (up to 100 nm) with low specific surface areas,[[10]] degrading the catalytic activities. 

Inspired by the motivation to exploit highly efficient bifunctional ORR/OER electrocatalysts via engineering the interfacial structure of composite catalysts, herein, an innovative strategy, based on low-intensity pulsed-laser irradiation (LI-PLI), is developed to controllably and selectively regulate the relative contents of pyridinic and pyrrolic N in NiCo2O4/N-graphene hybrids (NiCo2O4/NLG, or NiCo/NLG for short). As shown in **Scheme 1** , the LI-PLI method is capable of tuning the inplane mesopores density of the resulted mesoporous graphene oxides (LGO), thus in turn, facilitating engineering the relative concentrations of pyridinic and pyrrolic N during the subsequent hydrothermal treatments for nitrogen doping and small NiCo2O4 NPs growth. Strong chemical bonding between N dopants and Co ions were confirmed. The underlying correlation between different nitrogen configurations in CoNC bonds and the synergistically enhanced oxygen electrochemistry (ORR/OER) for the hybrid catalysts were experimentally investigated, and eventually, deep insights into the mechanism of the synergistic effect were acquired in combination of experiments and density functional theory (DFT) calculations. The   pyridinic-N Co bonding, instead of pyrrolic-N Co bonding, was identified as the key for the synergistically promoted ORR and OER. The best composite (NiCo/NLG-270) exhibits outstanding reversible ORR/OER performance, surpassing most of the reported bi-functional electrocatalysts, as well as a combination of benchmark noble-metal catalysts (Pt/C + RuO2). Furthermore, when applied as the air electrode for a rechargeable Zn–air battery, NiCo/NLG-270 demonstrates excellent charge/ discharge behavior and long-term stability superior to the noble-metal based electrocatalysts.

More importantly, _J_ K, OER is observed to be positively correlated to the pyridinic-N content (Figure 2f), but not the pyrrolic-N content, implying that pyridinic-NCo bonds contribute the most to the synergistic effect for OER. With an increasing pyridinic N content in NiCo/NLG-x, _J_ K, OER substantially augments up to 38 mA cm[−][2] for NiCo/NLG-270, twice that of NiCo/NG and surpassing that of RuO2. At last, Nyquist plot shows that NiCo/NLG-270 has much smaller interface electrontransfer resistance than the control samples (Figure S26, Supporting Information), implying that the pyridinic-NCo bonds afford much faster electron transfer, and in turn, promoting the OER process.

In contrast, RuO2 has much worse durability in 1 m KOH solution, with a significant drop in the current density after 10 h of polarization. 

To further theoretically elucidate the crucial role of pyridinicNCo bonds of NiCo/NLG composites in synergistic electrocatalysis for reversible ORR/OER, DFT calculations were conducted (see details in the Supporting Information). Chemical bonds between different types of nitrogen dopants (Figure S28, Supporting Information) and the surface cobalt atoms of NiCo2O4 as well as their influence on ORR/OER intrinsic activities were investigated. According to the theoretical model (Figure S29, Supporting Information), the surface Co are three-fold coordinated. Notably, in the case of pyridinicN-graphene, the surface Co atom is bonded to the pyridinic N with a bond length of 1.98 Å, and the differential charge density displays certain amount of electron transfer from Co to N in the NiCo/Pyri-NG composite (Figure S30a, Supporting Information), in good agreement with the observations in the XPS and EELS measurements (Figure 1e,g,h). The mod erate pyridinic-N Co bond strength and charge transfer will remarkably facilitate activity enhancement for reversible oxygen electrocatalysis in regards to the pure NiCo2O4 NPs, as demonstrated explicitly below. On the contrary, there is negligible electron transfer between pyrrolic N and Co, leading to a longer CoN bond length (2.15 Å) and very weak bonding strength  for pyrrolic-N Co (Figure S30b, Supporting Information). The Co-pyrrolic N bond is almost vertical to NiCo2O4 surface and the Co site is spatially confined by N doped graphene sheet, leaving no space for the adsorption of oxygen-containing intermediates. Meanwhile, the nearest Co is ≈3.15 Å away from the Co-pyrrolic N bond and is negligibly influenced by N doped graphene. Consequently, theoretically speaking, the pyrrolic-NCo bonding could not effectively improve the ORR/OER activities, consistent with the experimental observations. Besides, in the graphitic configuration, the excess valence electrons of N are delocalized, resulting in an n-type doping effect,[[19]] and making it difficult to form chemical bonds between graphitic N and Co due to electron repulsion (Figure S30c, Supporting Information). Interestingly, for the hybrid catalyst NiCo/PyriNG, pyridinic N, surface Co, and its neighboring Ni synergistically promote the efficiency of both ORR and OER processes. The proposed ORR/OER mechanisms on NiCo/Pyri-NG and plain NiCo2O4 are shown in **Figure 3** a and Figure S31 (Supporting Information), respectively. For Co sites on the surface of NiCo2O4, adsorptions of relevant species (O*, OH*, OO*, 

and OOH*) tend to compensate its three-fold coordination to form Co-centered tetrahedral (O* or OH*) or pyramidal (OO* or OOH*) crystal fields (Figure 3c(i) and Figure S30, Supporting Information). As we know, NiCo2O4 possesses the ground-state structure of inverse spinel, suggesting that fourfold coordination is stable for Co while six-fold for Ni, as proved by previous calculations based on its lowest cohesive energy.[[16a]] Consequently, for both ORR/OER over NiCo2O4, formation of four-fold coordinated Co is exothermic, whereas destruction of CoTd crystal field is energetically unfavorable, implying that the formation of OO* (ORR) and OOH* (OER) are endothermic (Figure 3c(i)). As expected, the calculated free energy diagram illustrates that the adsorption of O2 (Step , overpotential 0.34 V) and transition from O* to OOH* (Step , overpotential 0.50 V) are rate-limiting for ORR and OER in an alkaline solution (Figure 3b), respectively. On the contrary, when coordinated with pyridinic N, the interfacial Co atom is four-fold coordinated and thus stabilized in NiCo/Pyri-NG (Figure 3c(ii)). Therefore, reactions which weaken (strengthen) the bond between Co (Ni) and oxygen-containing intermediates are energetically favorable, due to the fact that Co tends to occupy the tetrahedral field in regards to octahedral field for Ni. Therefore, OO* (ORR) and OOH* (OER) will coadsorb on the Co site and its adjacent Ni site in NiCo/Pyri-NG (Figure 3a,c(ii)). DFT results indicate that, due to coadsorption, the formation of OO* (ORR) and OOH* (OER), which are rate-limiting steps for plain NiCo2O4, become spontaneously exothermic subreactions for NiCo/Pyri-NG (Figure 3b). In this case, protonation of OO* (overpotential 0.22 V) and desorption of O2 (overpotential 0.29 V) are the most sluggish steps for ORR and OER, respectively, and the overpotentials for reversible oxygen electrocatalysis are substantially reduced in comparison to NiCo2O4 alone. Furthermore, the pyridinic-NCo bond facilitates electron storage and transfer, which acts as a sponge to storage electrons. Oxygen-containing species (O*, OH*, OO*, OOH*) will then obtain electrons from both Co and pyridinic N after being adsorbed on the active sites, thus optimizing the adsorption of the intermediates and facilitating the reversible ORR/OER processes. Additionally, the introducing of Ni also leads to a half-metallic ferrimagnetic structure of NiCo2O4 in which the minority spin channel becomes conducting compared to insulated Co3O4.[[16a,20]] Thus NiCo/NLG-270 shows better ORR/OER activities than Co3O4/NLG-270 (Figure S32, Supporting Information). 

At last, we compare the overall reversible ORR and OER electrocatalytic performance of the NiCo/NLG-270 hybrid with the benchmark noble-metal catalysts Pt/C, RuO2, and the mixture Pt/C+RuO2 in alkaline condition, as shown in **Figure 4** a. It turns out that NiCo/NLG-270 exhibits better catalytic activity than the state-of-art precious-metal-based bifunctional catalyst Pt/C+RuO2.
